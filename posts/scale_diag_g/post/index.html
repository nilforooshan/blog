Scale G for inbreeding coefficients >= 0

August 8, 2025 · Mohammad Ali Nilforooshan

Inbreeding is the probability that an individual inherits two identical alleles from a common ancestor (Falconer and Mackay, 1996). There are various ways of obtaining inbreeding coefficients using pedigree and genomic information. One way is via the diagonal elements of the genetic relationship matrix. Denoting the numerator (pedigree) relationship matrix with A and the ith diagonal element with $a_{ii}$, the inbreeding coefficient of the ith animal equals:

$$ F_i = a_{ii} - 1. $$

The diagonal elements of A have a one-tailed distribution with a minimum of 1. Calculating inbreeding coefficients via the diagonal elements of the genomic relationship matrix (G):

$$ F_i = g_{ii} - 1, $$ some inbreeding coefficients are negative, because the diagonal elements of G have a two-tailed right-skewed distribution with some values less than 1.

Here, I introduce weights for scaling G to bring the minimum of its diagonal values to 1 and preserve the maximum of its diagonal values. The scaling restricts the genetic relationship of an animal with itself (i.e., the diagonal element of G) from going below 1, and the inbreeding coefficient from going below 0.

Consider vector g containing the $g_{ii}$ elements (i.e., diagonal elements of G). Then, multiplying $g_{ii}$ by $w_i$ weights, scales g to a range of 1 to max(g), where

$$ w_i = \frac{\frac{(g_{ii} - \text{min}(\mathbf g)) (\text{max}(\mathbf g) - 1)}{\text{max}(\mathbf g) - \text{min}(\mathbf g)} + 1}{g_{ii}}. $$

Collating $w_i$ elements into vector w, it is ranged from 1/min(g) (applied to min(g)) to 1 (applied to max(g)).

The following plots show the distribution of diag(G) before and after scaling. The blue and red lines show the median and the mean, respectively.

If G is needed as a whole, the rest of G should be scaled too. This can be achived by the $\sqrt{\mathbf W} \mathbf G \sqrt{\mathbf W}$ multiplication, where W is a diagonal matrix of $w_i$ elements.
